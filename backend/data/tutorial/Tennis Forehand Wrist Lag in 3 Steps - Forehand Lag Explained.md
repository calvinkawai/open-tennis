## 1. Video Summary

**Technical Detail:** 
*   Forehand lag is defined as delaying the racket head behind the grip and hand prior to hitting the ball, with the butt cap leading toward the contact point.
*   Locking the wrist back creates stability against the ball, prevents wrist injuries, and establishes mechanical leverage similar to hammering a nail.
*   This leverage produces a "whip effect," allowing the end of the racket to travel significantly faster than the grip to generate maximum power and topspin.
*   An effortless lag is achieved through three core steps: starting with a high racket head, transferring energy via a ground-up kinetic chain, and maintaining a loose grip.

## 2. Segments

**Step 1: High Racket Head**
Start the swing with the racket head higher than the grip, supporting the racket's weight with your non-hitting hand. This naturally pre-cocks the wrist and gives the racket space to drop, creating effortless acceleration.
*   **Key coaching cue:** "instant acceleration means instant power"
*   **Visual focus:** Watch the non-hitting hand holding the racket throat so the racket head stays above the grip height during preparation.

**Step 2: The Kinetic Chain**
Power transfers sequentially, requiring you to coil the trunk and separate your shoulder from your hip. To initiate the forward swing, drive up from the ground, lift the heel, and uncoil the knee, hip, and shoulder before the arm and wrist fire.
*   **Key coaching cue:** "it all starts from the ground up"
*   **Visual focus:** Watch the back heel lifting and the hips and shoulders uncoiling right before the racket snaps forward.

**Step 3: Wrist Relaxation**
A tight grip stiffens the arm and ruins lag, so keep the hitting hand loose by lifting your fingertips slightly off the grip during the backswing. Grip the handle securely only as you swing forward, and exhale through the shot to keep your entire body relaxed.
*   **Key coaching cue:** "breathe out through the shot"
*   **Visual focus:** Watch the relaxed fingertips releasing the grip during the backswing and the player actively exhaling at contact.

**Training Drills**
To feel the proper locked-back wrist position, practice pulling a towel or a coach-resisted racket out of the backswing "slot". To train the leverage, execute drop-feed drills where you attempt to strike the ball directly with the racket's butt cap instead of the strings.
*   **Key coaching cue:** "butt cap of the racket leading the way"
*   **Visual focus:** Watch the butt cap driving precisely toward the ball prior to the racket face whipping through.

*   Instruction: Loosen your fingers off the grip and let the racket drop for instant acceleration.
*   Visual: Racket falling into the slot, downward motion arrows, relaxed fingertips. ✗ Racket head and grip dropping on the same level.

**Step 3: Ground-Up Drive**
*   Instruction: Drive from the ground, lift your heel, and uncoil your hips and shoulders.
*   Visual: Back heel raised, upward motion arrow on leg, curved arrows uncoiling the hips. ✗ Swinging from the shoulder with a stiff arm.

**Step 4: Butt Cap Lead**
*   Instruction: Pull the butt cap toward the ball, locking your wrist back before contact.
*   Visual: Butt cap pointed directly at the ball, forward motion arrow, wrist fully laid back. ✗ Racket head and grip coming through together.
