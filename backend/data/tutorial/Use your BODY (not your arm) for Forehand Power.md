## 1. Video Summary

**Technical Detail:** 
*   **The core is the engine:** Forehand power originates from coiling and uncoiling the body's core, not from accelerating the arm.
*   **Unit turn separation:** A proper unit turn creates a physical separation between the alignment of the shoulders and the hips, building a rotational coil.
*   **Passive lag and snap:** Racket lag is a natural evolution of a relaxed arm being pulled by the body's rotation, not an active twisting or snapping of the wrist.
*   **Hand as steering wheel:** While the core generates the swing's power, the hand acts only as a steering wheel to direct the ball.
*   **Medicine ball training:** Throwing a medicine ball for distance forces the body to rely on core rotation rather than arm strength, teaching the correct kinetic feeling for groundstrokes.

## 2. Segments

**The Unit Turn and Coil**
Initiating the forehand requires turning the torso to misalign the shoulders and hips. This separation builds the necessary rotational tension before the forward swing begins.
*   **Key coaching cue:** "it creates a coil that he can then uncoil".
*   **Visual focus:** Watch the shoulders turn fully to the side while the hips remain partially open or facing inward.

**Uncoiling and Natural Lag**
The forward stroke begins by opening the hips, which pulls a completely relaxed arm and racket forward. The "lag" is a passive trailing effect, avoiding any forced wrist manipulation.
*   **Key coaching cue:** "the lag and snap isn't something that is created through turning your racket... but it's a natural evolution of using your body and being relaxed".
*   **Visual focus:** Watch the hips open up first, pulling the shoulders and leaving the racket trailing loosely behind.

**Contact and Extension**
At the point of contact, the uncoiling is complete, and the upper and lower body squarely face the net. The core drives this upward and forward acceleration.
*   **Key coaching cue:** "the engine of your swing is your core how you're using your body and the hand I consider to be the steering wheel".
*   **Visual focus:** Watch the forward-facing hips and shoulders at the moment of contact, with the racket accelerating through the ball.

**Medicine Ball Training**
Throwing a heavy object for maximum distance naturally trains the body to use core rotation rather than isolated arm strength. This physical sensation translates directly to an efficient tennis stroke.
*   **Key coaching cue:** "if you're using your body and coiling deeper to throw the ball farther you're going to start having to incorporate your body".
*   **Visual focus:** Watch the deep torso coil transferring energy into the throw, bypassing the arm muscles.

## 3. Cartoon Card (4 Steps)

**Step 1: The Unit Turn**
*   **Instruction:** Separate shoulders from hips during the turn to build your rotational coil.
*   **Visual:** Shoulders turned fully sideways, hips facing partially forward. **✗** Shoulders and hips aligned perfectly.

**Step 2: The Uncoil**
*   **Instruction:** Open hips forward first, letting the relaxed arm and racket lag passively behind.
*   **Visual:** Hips rotating forward, motion arrow on waist, racket trailing horizontally. **✗** Forcing the wrist backward actively.

**Step 3: Contact**
*   **Instruction:** Square hips and shoulders forward at impact, letting the core drive the racket.
*   **Visual:** Body squared directly to the net, racket striking the ball. **✗** Arm swinging far ahead of body rotation.

**Step 4: Practice Drill**
*   **Instruction:** Throw a medicine ball for distance to feel your core power the rotation.
*   **Visual:** Player in a deep coil throwing a heavy ball forward, rotational motion arrows. **✗** Tossing the ball strictly using arm muscles.
