## 1. Video Summary

**Technical Detail:** 
*   **Racket Head Speed:** Generating fast racket movement through the contact zone is the foundational element for producing effortless forehand power.
*   **Upper Body Coil:** Turn the non-dominant shoulder early to separate it from the hip, creating a deep twist that stores energy in the trunk and obliques.
*   **Circular Loop:** Lift the racket head higher than the grip during preparation to lengthen the swing path and create leverage, avoiding a forced, short swing.
*   **Racket Lag:** Relax the wrist completely so the racket head falls behind the hand, pointing the butt cap at the oncoming ball to maximize leverage prior to contact.
*   **Ground Force:** Lower the center of gravity by bending the knees, then use a neutral stance to shift weight from the back leg to the front leg for a linear, powerful drive.
*   **Shadow Swings:** Practice the entire kinetic chain without a ball, listening for an audible "swoosh" to confirm racket acceleration.

## 2. Segments

- **Racket Head Speed**
Fast racket movement through the contact zone is the ultimate source of forehand power. Every mechanical step in the preparation phase is designed solely to maximize this speed.
Key coaching cue: "everything starts and finishes with generating more racket head speed".
Visual focus: Shadow swing execution, listening for the audible swoosh sound through the contact zone.

- **Upper Body Coil**
Turn the non-dominant shoulder immediately upon recognizing a forehand to separate it from the hip. Move to the ball while holding this side-on position to store energy in the major trunk muscles.
Key coaching cue: "feel that my left shoulder is separating from my left hip".
Visual focus: A deep twist in the abdominal and oblique muscles, with the non-dominant arm stretching across the body.

- **Circular Swing Loop**
Elevate the racket head above the grip level during the takeaway to increase the physical distance between the power position and the contact point. This high-to-low path provides the necessary space to build relaxed acceleration.
Key coaching cue: "having a circular loop will help you to generate more power".
Visual focus: Racket head height relative to the grip height during the initial backswing.

- **Racket Lag**
Allow the wrist to naturally lay back so the racket head trails behind the grip during the forward swing. This creates a hammer-like leverage against the force of the oncoming ball.
Key coaching cue: "hit the ball with the bottom of the racket".
Visual focus: The butt cap of the racket pointing directly forward toward the oncoming ball.

- **Ground Force**
Engage the quads, hamstrings, glutes, and calves by dropping your overall height by about one foot. Use a neutral stance and drive linearly into the ball by transferring weight onto the front foot.
Key coaching cue: "weight shift back leg front leg".
Visual focus: Deep knee bend supporting a straight back, followed by a forward weight transfer.

## 3. Cartoon Card (4 Steps)

- **Step 1: The Coil**
Instruction: Turn your front shoulder early to separate from your hip and store trunk energy.
Visual: Shoulders turned sideways, front arm across chest, torso twisted, ✗ mark on front-facing chest.

- **Step 2: The Loop**
Instruction: Keep the racket head higher than your grip to create a longer acceleration path.
Visual: Racket raised high, circular motion arrow drawn down and forward, ✗ mark on low takeaway.

- **Step 3: The Lag**
Instruction: Relax your wrist and point the racket butt cap directly at the oncoming ball.
Visual: Wrist laid back, racket lagging behind hand, butt cap aiming forward, ✗ mark on stiff wrist.

- **Step 4: The Drive**
Instruction: Bend your knees and shift your weight from the back leg to the front leg.
Visual: Knees deeply bent, neutral stance, horizontal motion arrow pointing forward, ✗ mark on bending at waist.
