## 1. Video Summary

**Technical Detail:** 
*   **Grip Selection:** A semi-western, eastern, or western grip is required to allow the racket face to point away from the body and enable proper hand rotation; the continental grip prevents this.
*   **The Drop:** Prior to contact, the racket head must drop beneath the contact point.
*   **Pronation:** During the swing, the forearm must pronate, shifting from a supinated (palm facing the sky) position to a pronated position.
*   **Wrist Release:** The wrist begins in a laid-back position but must release into a neutral position simultaneously with forearm pronation.
*   **Contact and Path:** The ball is struck with a flat racket face that immediately brushes up the back of the ball from low to high, leading with the top edge of the racket.
*   **The Finish:** The racket head travels from pointing down before contact to pointing up toward the sky after contact, generating heavy topspin for net clearance and dipping action.

## 2. Segments

**Segment 1: Selecting the Right Grip**
The foundation of the windshield wiper forehand is choosing a grip that naturally allows the hand to rotate and the racket face to point away from the body at contact. The continental grip forces a tense upward motion, whereas the semi-western grip provides the ideal balance for generating topspin.
*   Key coaching cue: "the semi-western is the optimal position".
*   Visual focus: Watch the racket face pointing away from the body and the hand position on the grip.

**Segment 2: Forearm Pronation and Wrist Action**
To create the "wiper" effect, the forearm must actively roll over while striking the ball. A critical technical milestone is ensuring the wrist does not stay locked in a laid-back position, but releases into a neutral alignment during pronation.
*   Key coaching cue: "have the wrist come out of that position into a neutral position while you're pronating".
*   Visual focus: Watch the forearm rotate from palm-up to palm-down, and the wrist shift from laid-back to neutral.

**Segment 3: Swing Path and Racket Face**
The steepness of the upward swing determines the amount of topspin generated. The racket must meet the ball flat to grip the strings, but the top edge of the frame immediately leads across and closes off to brush the ball violently from low to high.
*   Key coaching cue: "the ball is always going to be met with a flat racket face so that i can brush the back of that ball".
*   Visual focus: Watch the top edge of the racket frame leading the rotation around the hand toward the shoulder.

**Segment 4: Windshield Wiper Drills**
The technique is built through progressive rolling drills to isolate the low-to-high brushing motion. This starts with hand-rolling the ball over the net, progressing to rolling it with the racket, then drop feeds focusing purely on the low-to-high path, and finally full-body integrated strokes.
*   Key coaching cue: "focusing on getting that racket path as high as possible so low to high".
*   Visual focus: Watch the racket start below the ball and finish with the racket head pointing directly to the sky.

## 3. Cartoon Card (4 Steps)

**Step 1: The Grip**
Hold the racket with a semi-western grip to allow for maximum hand rotation.
*   Visual: Hand gripping the handle with semi-western alignment; racket face pointing away from the body. ✗ Continental grip.

**Step 2: The Drop**
Drop the racket head completely beneath the contact point with a laid-back wrist.
*   Visual: Body low, wrist flexed back, racket head pointing down below the incoming ball. ✗ Racket head floating above the ball.

**Step 3: Contact & Pronation**
Pronate the forearm over and move the wrist to a neutral position at contact.
*   Visual: Forearm rotating palm-down, racket face flat against the ball, upward motion arrows. ✗ Wrist frozen tightly in a laid-back position.

**Step 4: The Wiper Finish**
Brush up the back of the ball and finish with the racket tip up.
*   Visual: Racket top edge leading around the hand, tip pointing to the sky, low-to-high arrows. ✗ Pushing the racket straight and flat through the ball.
